What “stereo validation” can mean here

Learners draw arrows, never products: the app applies the arrows and compares the result with the authored next state. So stereochemistry can only go wrong in three places.

KindExampleLearner’s choice?What is needed
A. Outcome onlySN2 inversion; anti opening of a bromonium ion; syn hydroboration; thermal con/disrotatoryNo: the arrows are the same whatever the stereoThe authored states carry stereo and are right; the learner sees it drawn correctly

A bug today. In an E2, removing the H that is syn to the bromine is accepted as correct: the app treats every H on a carbon as interchangeable. Phase 2 fixes this.

Phase 1: stereo in the states (kind A)

  • Stereo is written in the state’s SMILES (@, @@ for a stereocentre; /, \ for a double bond). Today these marks are refused.
  • Wedges stay the drawing, and the linter checks they agree with the SMILES. The editor fills the wedges in when it generates a state.
  • Applying arrows keeps every stereocentre the arrows do not touch. A centre the step makes or changes takes its configuration from the authored next state: you decide inversion, anti addition and so on.
  • New lint checks: wedges that disagree with the SMILES (error); a stereocentre that changes although no arrow touches it (error); an unmarked stereocentre in a state that marks others (warning).
  • An alternative product may be a stereoisomer (E/Z, a diastereomer), with a caption.
  • Racemic outcomes (SN1, addition to a flat C=O): one enantiomer drawn, caption “racemic”.

Phase 2: telling diastereotopic atoms apart (kind B)

  • Two atoms count as interchangeable only if swapping them keeps every stereocentre and double-bond geometry. The two H on C3 of 2-bromobutane, or the axial and equatorial H of a drawn chair, become different atoms.
  • Choosing the wrong one is then a wrong answer, or an authored misconception: “This H is syn to the bromine: E2 needs it anti-periplanar.”
  • Mirror-image H in an achiral molecule stay interchangeable. The exact rule is fixed in test cases before coding.
  • Drawing and tapping need no change: those H are already separate atoms on screen.

Phase 3: choosing a face (kind C) — needs your decision

Recommended: a product question after the arrows. When the author gives a step one or more stereoisomeric alternatives (each with a feedback message), a learner who drew the right arrows sees the candidate products side by side, “Which product forms?”, and taps one. A wrong pick shows the authored message and counts as a wrong check. It reuses the drawings and needs no 3D.

Other options: a “from the front / from behind” toggle on the arrow (hard to show on a flat drawing), or explanation only, as today.

Build order

Each step test-first, starting from shared fixtures.

  1. Reading and writing stereo marks in state SMILES.
  2. Applying arrows keeps untouched stereo; test cases for SN2 inversion, bromonium anti opening and E2.
  3. Stereo-aware comparison and the lint checks; the editor fills wedges.
  4. Stereo-aware symmetry; E2 test cases (the anti H right, the syn H wrong, a misconception on it).
  5. Content: add stereo to the SN2, E2 and bromination drafts as the first users.
  6. Phase 3 after your decision and a mockup.
